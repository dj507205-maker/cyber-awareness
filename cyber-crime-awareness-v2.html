Common cyber crimes

Select a crime to see how it works and how to avoid it.

Phishing and fake links

Fraudsters send SMS, email or WhatsApp messages that look like they come from a bank, courier or government office. The link opens a copy of the real site and steals your login, card number or OTP.

Never open links from unknown senders. Type the bank's address yourself or use its official app.

OTP and UPI fraud

A caller pretends to be from your bank or an "offer" and asks for an OTP, PIN, or asks you to approve a "collect request". Approving a request sends money out of your account.

You never enter a PIN to receive money. No bank asks for your OTP or PIN.

Digital arrest and fake officer calls

Scammers pose as police, customs or courier staff, say you are involved in a crime and keep you on a video call to force you to transfer money. Real agencies do not arrest people over video call or ask for payment to "settle" a case.

Hang up, do not pay, and call 1930.

Fake jobs and investment schemes

Offers of easy money for liking videos, part-time tasks or "guaranteed" trading returns. Early small payouts build trust, then victims are asked to deposit larger amounts that never come back.

Genuine jobs never ask you to pay a fee. Guaranteed high returns are a warning sign.

Social media impersonation and sextortion

Criminals clone a friend's profile to ask for urgent money, or trick people into private video chats and then threaten to leak recordings. Both rely on fear and urgency.

Verify by calling the person. Do not pay a blackmailer. Save the chat and report it.

Malware and fake apps

Apps or files sent through chat (for example an "APK" for a challan, KYC or a free gift) can read your SMS and control your phone.

Install apps only from the official Play Store or App Store, and keep updates on.

If you are scammed, act fast

Money reported quickly has a better chance of being frozen. Do these in order.

  1. Call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) right away and give the transaction details.
  2. Call your bank to block the card, UPI or net banking and ask them to flag the transaction.
  3. File a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in with screenshots, phone numbers, UPI IDs and transaction IDs.
  4. Keep the evidence. Do not delete chats, call logs or messages. Change your passwords.

Habits that keep you safe

Small routines that block most attacks.

  • Use a different strong password for each important account and a password manager.
  • Turn on two-step verification for email, social media and banking.
  • Pause when a message creates panic or a deadline. Scammers depend on speed.
  • Share OTPs, PINs and CVV with nobody, including "bank staff".
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for banking and keep your phone and apps updated.
  • Check the sender's number, web address and spelling before you trust a message.
  • Keep social media profiles private and limit personal details you post.
  • Tell family members, especially elders, about these tricks.

Laws that protect you

Cyber crimes are punishable in India. These are some of the main provisions. Check official sources for the full text.

ProvisionWhat it covers
IT Act 2000, Section 43Unauthorised access to a computer or data, and damage to it. Compensation can be claimed.
IT Act, Section 66CIdentity theft, such as misusing someone's password or digital signature.
IT Act, Section 66DCheating by pretending to be someone else using a computer or phone.
IT Act, Section 66EViolating privacy by capturing or sharing private images.
IT Act, Section 67Publishing or sending obscene material electronically.
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Section 318Cheating, which applies to many online frauds.
